Word Knowledge

Vocabulary, context clues, prefixes and roots — explanations and worked examples to complement your practice questions.

Vocabulary in context

A familiar word may have different meanings in different sentences. Read the complete question and determine its part of speech. Concise means brief but clear; reluctant means hesitant or unwilling; vigilant means watchful or alert.

For synonyms, ask whether a possible answer could replace the word without changing the overall meaning of a sentence.

Useful word parts

Prefixes and roots help you infer unfamiliar words. Pre- means before (preview), inter- means between (interact), sub- means under (submarine), and anti- means against (antibiotic).

Suffixes can reveal word type: -less often means without (careless), while -ology means the study of a subject (biology).

Elimination strategy

Eliminate choices that have the wrong emotional tone, intensity, or part of speech. For instance, arduous describes something demanding or difficult—not simply long, unusual, or boring. Two answers may be loosely related, but only one is the closest synonym.

Daily practice

Build a personal list of 10 unfamiliar words each day. Write a short definition, an original sentence, and one near-synonym. Review the words a day later and again at the end of the week. Short, repeated retrieval practice is more helpful than rereading a long word list once.
