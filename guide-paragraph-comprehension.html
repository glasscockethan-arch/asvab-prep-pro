Paragraph Comprehension

Main ideas, factual detail, inference and tone — explanations and worked examples to complement your practice questions.

Main idea versus supporting detail

The main idea is the central point the whole passage supports. Details give examples or evidence. Consider: “The library extended its weekend hours after surveys showed more students wanted quiet study space. Attendance rose over the following month.” The main idea is that extended hours helped meet demand for study space, not merely that a survey occurred.

Evidence-based inference

An inference must be supported by the passage without adding outside assumptions. If a passage says a town installed barriers after repeated flooding, you may infer the town was trying to reduce flood damage. You cannot conclude the barriers eliminated all flooding unless the text supports that.

Purpose and tone

Informational passages explain or describe. Persuasive passages argue for a conclusion. Compare factual language with emotionally loaded wording. If asked about the author's purpose, choose what the author is trying to accomplish—not simply the topic the passage mentions.

Practice method

Read the question, read the passage carefully, then restate the answer in your own words before looking at the options. Return to the specific sentence that supports your choice. Watch for answers that are plausible in the real world but not supported by the text.
