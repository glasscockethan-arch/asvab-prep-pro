Sharpen the skills behind every course.

College learners need confident numerical reasoning, critical reading, research evaluation, and clear communication. These are introductory learning exercises, not accredited college credit or exam equivalencies.

Quantitative literacy: interpret percentages carefully

A change from 40% to 50% is 10 percentage points, but it represents a 25% relative increase because (50 − 40) ÷ 40 = 0.25. Always identify whether an article reports absolute change, percentage points, or relative change.

Statistics: understand the mean and median

For the set 2, 3, 3, 4, 18, the mean is (2+3+3+4+18) ÷ 5 = 6, while the median is 3. The unusually high 18 pulls the mean upward. The median may better represent the typical observation in skewed data.

Research literacy: evaluate a claim

Look for the author, publication date, primary evidence, methodology, sample size, and possible conflicts of interest. A correlation between two measurements does not by itself prove that one causes the other. Seek independent supporting evidence before accepting strong claims.
